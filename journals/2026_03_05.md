- In life, we often experience profound events that shape us as beings, and as a result we feel the need to hold onto some form of physical reminder of that event. Often, these are photographs, souvenirs, nick-knacks, and more. Such objects often evoke a strong emotional response, harkening back to the associated moments. For many, parting with such objects are devastating. For some, grief or distress rises when these items are lost, broken, or damaged. Sometimes, items that have no emotional ties provoke these feelings too -- such as the far to common experience of 'losing ones keys'. Through the teachings of the Dharma, it is possible to learn to overcome such suffering, and uproot the origin of it entirely. To understand how, one must fist understand a central truth of the Dharma.
- Impermanence echos through all things -- including all that is physical, mental, or spiritual. Seasons change, the leaves grow and fall; people age and become ripe with life. For some, this change is scary, and they cling to memories as well as momento from the past. They see this holding as a means to shield themselves from change, and as a result deny themselves the opportunity to find meaning in change.
- Admittedly, I also have fallen victim to this, and so today I am giving up something I have held dearly for a few years now. Something that represented a point of change in my life, to which I clung to as a reminder of that change. Though it pains me very much to give up this thing, I realize that I have an unnecessary attachment to it. Perhaps it is the fear of forgetting the significance of that change which drove me to hold onto it for so long. But through the Dharma I have realized that by holding onto tokens from the past, I am preventing myself from manifesting change within.
